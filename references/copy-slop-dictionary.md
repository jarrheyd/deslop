# Copy Slop Dictionary

The complete catalog of AI writing tells. Organized by type, with detection rules and fixes.

## Banned Phrases (Zero Tolerance)

These phrases are never acceptable in any context. They are the most recognized AI tells and should be caught by the hook.

### Opening/Transition Phrases
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "In the ever-evolving landscape of..."
- "In an era where..."
- "In this article/guide/blog post, we will..."
- "Have you ever wondered...?"
- "Are you struggling with...?"
- "What if I told you..."
- "Let's dive in / Let's dive deep / Let's unpack this"
- "Let's break it down"
- "Here's the thing..."
- "Here's why that matters"
- "At its core..."
- "It's worth noting that..."
- "It is important to note that..."
- "The bottom line is..."
- "In conclusion..."
- "Without further ado..."

### The "Not X, but Y" Family
The single most recognized AI pattern. Ban entirely.
- "It's not just X. It's Y."
- "It's not just about X — it's about Y."
- "X isn't just Y. It's Z."
- "This isn't just a tool. It's a revolution."
- Any reframe where the second clause inflates the first

### Aspirational Fluff
- "Unlock the power of..."
- "Unlock the potential of..."
- "Harness the power of..."
- "Elevate your workflow"
- "Revolutionize the way you..."
- "Transform how you..."
- "Reimagine what's possible"
- "Seamlessly integrate..."
- "Take your X to the next level"
- "Empower your team to..."
- "Supercharge your..."
- "Game-changer" / "Game-changing"

### Sycophantic Responses
- "Great question!"
- "Absolutely!"
- "I love that idea!"
- "That's a fantastic point!"
- "Excellent observation!"

### Filler Acknowledgments
- "And that matters."
- "And honestly?"
- "And here's the kicker."
- "Think about that for a second."
- "Read that again."
- "Let that sink in."

### Two-Word Wisdom Closers
- "Worth it."
- "Math checks out."
- "It tracks."
- "Make it make sense."
- "End of."

### Manufactured-Candor Openers
- "Honestly,"
- "Genuinely,"
- "Real talk."
- "I'll be honest,"
- "Here's the truth."
- "Let me be real."

### Inline Section-Header Substitutes
The pattern "Word: rest of sentence" used as pseudo-heading mid-paragraph:
- "The question: ..."
- "The setup: ..."
- "The result: ..."
- "The catch: ..."
- "The kicker: ..."

### Rhetorical-Question Pile-Ups
Stacking two or three short rhetorical questions with no answer is an AI tic:
- "Top? Bottom? Both ends?"
- "What did you build? Why? For whom?"
- "Did it work? At what cost? Was it worth it?"

### "Not X. Not Y." Negation Ladders
The three-beat parallel negation, ending in a punchline:
- "Not 95. Not 98. Fifty for fifty."
- "Not a year. Not six months. Six weeks."
- "Not the model. Not the prompt. The harness."

### Either-Or Reductive Framing
- "Either it matches or it doesn't."
- "Either you ship or you don't."
- "Either X or Y." used to collapse nuance

### Generic Abstract Metaphors
- "Wherever it lives"
- "Moves the needle"
- "The right lever"
- "The missing piece"
- "Picking up steam"
- "Hits different"
- "The dial"

### "It came back X" passive constructions
- "It came back null."
- "It came back surprising."
- "It came back exactly as predicted."

### TikTok-Aphorism Patterns
- "X but make it Y"
- "If you know, you know"
- "iykyk" / "ick"
- "X is the new Y"

### Hyphenated-Compound Titles
Over-compounded noun phrases used as titles:
- "the-position-of-the-rule test"
- "the-thing-that-quietly-broke story"
- "the-X-that-Y framing"

### Structural / formula opening patterns
- "In today's [adjective] digital [noun]"
- "The [adjective] world of [topic]"
- "In an era of [X]"
- "When it comes to [X]"
- Bolded one-line opening statements followed by explanation

### Stylistic tells (density-based)
- **Uniform sentence length** — AI writes at one rhythm; humans vary widely
- **Passive voice dominance** — "is conducted by", "was implemented", "has been observed" stacked together
- **Lack of contractions** — never "I'm" / "you're" / "that's"; always full "I am" / "you are"
- **Hedging soup** — "one might argue", "it could be said that", "some would say"
- **All transition words at paragraph starts** — Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, in sequence
- **Sycophantic em-dash interjections** — "— and this is the kicker — "

## Flagged Words (Density-Based)

These words aren't banned — they're real English words. But AI uses them at 10-50x the frequency of human writers. Flag when they appear more than once per 500 words.

### Tier 1: Strongest AI Signals
These words are almost never used by human writers in professional/marketing contexts but appear constantly in AI output.

**Verbs**: delve, utilize, spearhead, bolster, underscore, endeavor, foster, harness, streamline, leverage

**Adjectives**: pivotal, multifaceted, holistic, meticulous, nuanced, invaluable, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, robust, seamless

**Nouns**: tapestry, synergy, paradigm, underpinnings, linchpin, cornerstone, catalyst, nexus, realm, landscape (metaphorical)

**Adverbs**: "quietly" (as in "quietly revolutionary"), "arguably," "fundamentally," "inherently"

### Tier 2: Moderate AI Signals
Common enough in human writing but over-indexed by AI. Flag at 2+ per 500 words.

**Verbs**: navigate, elevate, optimize, showcase, highlight, emphasize, enhance, unlock, empower, revolutionize, curate

**Adjectives**: transformative, comprehensive, crucial, vital, dynamic, compelling, innovative, robust, vibrant, profound

**Nouns**: ecosystem, framework, trajectory, benchmark, intersection

**Transitions**: furthermore, moreover, consequently, notably, importantly, indeed, additionally, conversely, nevertheless, that said, it bears noting

### Tier 3: Context-Dependent
Not AI tells on their own but become tells in combination or at high frequency.

**Nouns**: journey (metaphorical), space (as in "the AI space"), stakeholders, deep dive, thought leadership, value proposition

**Phrases**: "moving forward," "at the end of the day," "when it comes to," "in terms of," "the fact that"

## Era-Specific Vocabulary (What's Hot Now)

AI vocabulary shifts as models are retrained and post-processing filters evolve.

| Era | Signature Words |
|-----|----------------|
| 2023-early 2024 | "delve" was THE tell |
| Mid 2024-mid 2025 | "bolstered," "crucial," "emphasizing" dominated |
| Late 2025-2026 | "emphasizing," "enhance," "highlighting," "showcasing" — "delve" dropped sharply |

**Why this matters**: If your copy uses the current era's AI vocabulary at density, it reads as freshly generated. Update your awareness as models shift.

## Structural Tells

These are harder to catch with keyword matching but equally damning.

### Em Dash Abuse (—)
AI uses em dashes as dramatic pauses at 5-10x human frequency. "The future of marketing — and it's closer than you think — is here."
- **Rule**: Maximum 1 em dash per 300 words of prose. Prefer commas, periods, or sentence restructuring.
- **Exception**: Em dashes in technical writing to set off parenthetical clauses are fine at moderate density.

### Uniform Paragraph Length
AI produces eerily balanced paragraphs — every paragraph approximately the same word count, same sentence count, same rhythm. Human writing is irregular: a 3-word paragraph followed by a 50-word paragraph.
- **Rule**: If all paragraphs in a piece are within 15% of the same word count, flag it.
- **Fix**: Vary deliberately. One-sentence paragraphs. Long flowing paragraphs. The irregularity IS the human signal.

### The Topic-Summary Sandwich
Every AI paragraph opens with a topic sentence and closes with a summary sentence. Real paragraphs often start in the middle of an idea, end abruptly, or let the reader draw the conclusion.
- **Rule**: If every paragraph has a clear thesis-first, summary-last structure, flag it.
- **Fix**: Start some paragraphs with examples. End some mid-thought. Trust the reader.

### Symmetric Bullet Points
AI bullet points are suspiciously uniform: same grammatical structure, same length, same pattern. Real bullet points are ragged.
- **Rule**: If all bullets are within 20% of the same word count and follow identical grammatical patterns, flag it.
- **Fix**: Let bullets be uneven. Some short. Some longer with sub-detail. Not every bullet needs the same structure.

### The Warm-Up Problem
AI repeats the obvious first (definition, why it matters, benefits) before getting to substance. The first 40% of AI content is throat-clearing.
- **Rule**: Start with the point. If the first substantive insight doesn't appear until paragraph 3+, cut everything before it.

### False Balance / Hedging
AI is terrified of taking a position. Everything is "nuanced" with "on one hand / on the other hand." Every claim is qualified with "arguably" or "it depends."
- **Rule**: Take a stance. If the piece hedges every point, it reads as AI trying not to offend. Specificity includes having an opinion.

### The Redundant Third Item
"Fast, efficient, and optimized for peak performance." The third item in AI lists always inflates into a longer phrase that means the same as the first two.
- **Rule**: If you list three things, each must add genuinely new information. If the third is the first two wearing a trenchcoat, cut it.

### Voiceless, Impersonal Tone
Correct and readable but with zero sense of a fallible, passionate human behind it. No opinions, no humor, no personality.
- **Rule**: Every piece of prose should pass the "who wrote this?" test. If the answer could be "anyone" or "no one," it needs voice.

### The Dramatic One-Word Sentence
"Know what else? / Typing like this. / The short sentences. / As if they're more special. / Impactful." Stacking single-sentence lines for false drama. Pure AI voice.
- **Rule**: One-word or one-phrase sentences work occasionally for emphasis. Three in a row is a tell.

### "You've been verbing. Verbing adverbly."
The pattern of [statement]. [Restatement with adverb]. Repetitive structure that sounds profound but says nothing new.
- **Rule**: If the second sentence restates the first with just an adverb added, delete it.

### 19 Paragraphs of Pontificating
AI generates volume, not density. Every paragraph should earn its place.
- **Rule**: If you can delete a paragraph and lose nothing, delete it. Aim for half the length, twice the substance.

## What to Do Instead

### Vary Sentence Rhythm
Short sentence. Then a longer one that takes its time. Then back to short. AI writes at one speed. Humans accelerate and brake.

### Use Contractions
"We're" not "We are." "Don't" not "Do not." "It's" not "It is." Formal register is AI's comfort zone. Contractions signal human.

### Be Specific Over Abstract
Not "many companies struggle with retention." Instead: "We lost 3 enterprise accounts in Q2 because onboarding took 6 weeks." Numbers, names, dates, tools, failures.

### Reference Real Things
Details that only someone who was there would know. The name of the coffee shop where the idea started. The Slack message that changed the direction. The exact error message that took 4 hours to debug.

### Start with the Point
Cut the introduction. Cut the context-setting. Cut "Before we dive in." Lead with the insight, the claim, or the story.

### Earn Every Paragraph
If you can delete a paragraph and the piece loses nothing, delete it. AI generates volume. Humans generate density.

### Have an Opinion
"X is better than Y because Z." Not "X and Y both have merits, and the best choice depends on your specific needs." Take a position. Back it up. Let the reader disagree.

## Carried from unslop (2026-08-18) — judgment patterns the hook can't regex

These need a reader, not a regex. The review gate and slop-detector agent apply them. The hook already blocks/warns the regex-able cousins (vague attributions, wordy phrases, false ranges, disclaimers, curly quotes, Title Case headings, bold overuse, "serves as").

- **Name-dropping without context.** Dropping a tool, company, framework, or person as if the reader already knows why it matters ("built on Kubernetes, powered by React, backed by leading VCs"). Fix: say what it does for this reader, or cut it. **Jarrhey exception:** his own internal shorthand (Gee, Chaye, HealthHub, "the pod") is NOT name-dropping — he and his readers know these. This pattern is for external/marketing copy only.
- **Hollow "-ing" openers.** Gerund-led sentences that front-load a promotional verb: "Leveraging cutting-edge AI, the platform delivers...", "Empowering teams to...". Fix: lead with the subject and a plain verb — "The platform does X."
- **Elegant variation.** Calling the same thing three different names in one passage to avoid repetition ("the tool… the solution… the platform… the offering"). Reads as padding. Fix: pick one name and repeat it. Repetition is clearer than variety here.
- **False ranges.** "Everything from X to Y" where X and Y don't bound a real span ("everything from startups to enterprises", "from strategy to execution"). Fix: name the actual items, or cut the frame. (Hook warns on the literal "everything from … to …".)
- **Decorative emojis.** Emoji used as bullet points, section markers, or sprinkled for tone in prose (✨🚀🔥 leading list items). Fix: cut them; let the words carry it. **Jarrhey exception:** the EA brief's fixed section/area emojis (🎯 NEEDS YOU, ⚖️ DECISIONS, 🏥 HealthHub, etc.) are an intentional, stable scanning system he asked for — NOT decoration. Never flag those. The tell is random/ornamental emoji, not his consistent header set.
- **Redundant labeled lists / unnecessary colons.** Every list item forced into "**Label:** sentence" when the label adds nothing, or a colon dropped mid-sentence for false weight ("The result: it worked"). Fix: if the label just restates the sentence, drop it; write the sentence. (His legitimate labeled lists — "Pending:", bold client-summary labels — are fine; the tell is labels that carry no information.)
- **Insincere agreement / manufactured candor mid-text.** "You're absolutely right to ask", "Great point", "Honestly," "To be fair" used as filler inside prose, not just as openers. Fix: cut; get to the content.

## Unslop-merge patterns (added 2026-08-27)

Ported from the Cursor "unslop" skill. The first four are enforced by the hook; the last two need human judgment.

- Weak copulas. "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features a". Say what it is or does: "is", "runs", "handles", "has". (hook: warn)
- Empty -ing tails. A trailing participle that adds nothing: ", highlighting the...", ", ensuring...", ", reflecting...", ", underscoring...". Cut it, or start a new sentence with the concrete point. (hook: warn)
- False ranges. "from X to Y" or "everything from X to Y" for two things that aren't a real spectrum. List the actual items or cut. (hook: warn)
- Abstract jargon-as-technical. "substrate", "nexus", "paradigm", "flywheel", "north star", "vector" (when non-technical). Name the concrete thing. (hook: warn; "vector" is judgment-only, legit in math/security)
- Synonym cycling. Rotating "the tool / the platform / the solution / the system" for one thing to avoid repetition. Pick one term and repeat it. Repetition reads as clarity, not as a mistake. (judgment)
- Colon overuse as a mid-sentence connector. "The reason is simple: it works" stacked repeatedly. Fine once; a tell at density. Use a period or a plain clause. (judgment)

The other half of the merge lives in `agents/copy-humanizer.md`: removing slop is only half the job, so the humanizer now has an explicit "add voice" step (de-sterilize) because sterile, voiceless prose is its own tell.

## From Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" (judgment tells the hook can't regex)

These need a reader. The hook already blocks the regex-able cousins (tool-remnant markers, chatbot outros, significance puffery, curly quotes, title case, weak copulas).

- **AI tool-remnant markers.** Literal leakage from the generating tool left in text: `:contentReference[oaicite:...]`, `[cite: 12]`, `contentReference`, `turn0search1`-style tokens. Instant tell, always delete. (hook: block)
- **Canned notability / media name-drop.** Proving importance by listing outlets instead of showing it: "featured in Vogue, Wired, and other publications". Cut, or state the one fact that matters. (judgment)
- **Undue significance / legacy framing.** "marked a pivotal moment in the evolution of...", "stands as a testament to...", "cemented its legacy". Overstatement standing in for evidence. Show the specific thing; drop the epitaph. (hook blocks the set phrases; judgment for the rest)
- **Outline-like conclusions.** The formulaic closer "Despite its success, X faces challenges... Looking ahead, future prospects...". A shape with no content. Cut it or replace with a concrete next fact. (judgment)
- **"Awards and Recognition" (and other "X and Y") section fixation.** AI reaches for legacy/recognition sections and paired-noun headings. Keep a section only if it carries real, sourced substance. (judgment)
- **Section summaries.** A recap sentence bolted to the end of every section ("In summary, the project..."). Trust the reader; delete. (judgment)
- **Heading-structure tells.** Headings that contain only other headings (no body), skipped levels (H2 to H4), several H1s in one document, or thematic-break rules sprinkled between sections. Structural residue of generation, not authored hierarchy. (judgment)
- **Collaborative / meta commentary.** Text addressing the reader or a reviewer directly, knowledge-cutoff disclaimers ("as of my last update"), or leftover placeholder/prompt text. None belong in finished copy. (hook blocks the common outros; judgment for the rest)
- **Vague connection terms.** "in connection with", "in relation to", "with regard to" where a plain preposition (about, for, with) is exact. (hook: warn)

**Signs of HUMAN writing (the counter-signal - presence of these argues AGAINST slop):** the text predates late 2022; the author can explain why a specific choice was made; idiosyncratic, slightly irregular syntax; concrete references only someone present would know. Absence of every tell is not proof of a human - but these are what real authorship leaves behind. See `references/what-humans-do.md`.

## Formatting habits & eyebrows (2026-08-27)

- **Eyebrows / kickers.** A short ALL-CAPS or label line stuck above a headline ("THE PROBLEM", "INTRODUCING", "WHY IT MATTERS"). AI reaches for these to signal structure. Delete it; the headline carries itself.
- **Random line breaks.** AI drops a line break mid-thought, or strands a single sentence on its own line for false weight. If the break is not a real paragraph or a real list item, it is decoration - close it up.
- **Stray leading dash / rule.** A dash or horizontal rule at the top of a section or slide as ornament. Cut it.
- **Explainer top-and-tail.** A header that restates what a section/slide is, plus a footer caption that re-explains it. Both are padding; the content is the explanation.

## Email & scannability tells (from a working email playbook)

For emails and any copy a busy person skims:
- **One takeaway per email.** If it has two asks, it is two emails.
- **Body supports the ONE main point** - every line earns its place or is cut.
- **Specific headlines**, not generic openers ("Quick update", "Hope you're well", "I wanted to reach out").
- **Cut unnecessary info; be punchy, not padded.** Limit to 1-3 key points.
- **Write for skimmers:** scannable, no blocks of text, 2-3 scrolls maximum.
- **Subject line: short and concise.** Preview text: one sentence that ADDS info, does not repeat the subject.
- **No fabricated claims** - never assert a number, outcome, or fact you cannot back.
- **No two-beat antithesis** ("it's not X, it's Y") and **no aphorism formulas** ("X, but make it Y", "X is the new Y"). Already blocked by the hook; called out here because they are the ones that slip into "polished" copy.
