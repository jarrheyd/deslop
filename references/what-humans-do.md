# What Humans Do — Counter-Patterns to AI Slop

Anti-slop tells you what to avoid. This file tells you what to do instead. These are the signals of authenticity across every medium — the things that make content feel like a human made it.

## The Core Principle

AI produces the most statistically probable output. Humans produce the most contextually appropriate output. The difference is **specificity** — AI generalizes, humans particularize.

Every counter-pattern below is a form of specificity: specific words, specific choices, specific references, specific imperfections.

## Writing That Feels Human

### Rhythm Variation
AI writes at one speed. Humans accelerate and brake. A 3-word sentence followed by a 40-word sentence is a human fingerprint.

Not this: "We built this tool to help teams collaborate better. Our platform enables seamless communication across departments. The result is improved productivity and alignment."

This: "We built this because our standups were 45 minutes long and nobody remembered what was decided. Now they're 12 minutes. The bot posts the summary before you close the Zoom tab."

### Contractions and Informality
AI defaults to formal register. "We are pleased to announce" instead of "We're launching." "It is important to consider" instead of "Think about." Contractions signal human.

### Specific References
Details only someone who was there would know. Not "we faced challenges" but "the deploy failed at 2am on a Thursday and the only person with production access was on a plane to Tokyo."

### Opinions With Teeth
Not "both approaches have merits." Instead: "Server-side rendering is the right default for 90% of apps, and if you chose a SPA in 2026, you should have a strong reason." Taking a position is human. Hedging is AI.

### Earned Brevity
Saying less, meaning more. AI pads content with qualifiers, transitions, and restatements. Humans who know their subject can be terse because the confidence shows.

Not this: "It is worth noting that the implementation of this particular feature required us to carefully consider multiple approaches before ultimately deciding on the most appropriate solution."

This: "We tried three approaches. Two were slow. The third worked."

### Voice Leaks
The moments where personality breaks through despite the format. A dry aside in documentation. A self-deprecating note in a changelog. Humor that's specific to the team's experience.

## Design That Feels Human

### Intentional Imperfection
A slightly unconventional spacing choice. An unexpected color accent. An asymmetric layout where symmetry was expected. These signal "a human decided this." In 2026, imperfection is the premium signal.

### Constraint as Personality
Fewer colors used well > many colors used generically. A restricted palette forces choices. Choices reveal personality. AI uses every color available because it doesn't know which to leave out.

### Negative Space as Decision
What you DON'T show matters. AI fills every pixel. A designer who leaves deliberate emptiness is making a statement: this content doesn't need decoration. The space itself communicates confidence.

### Real Content from Day One
Design with actual copy, actual data, actual screenshots. Lorem ipsum hides slop. Real 4-word headlines break layouts differently than 8-word placeholders. Design around truth, not ideals.

### States as Personality
The empty state, the error message, the loading indicator, the 404 page — these are where brand personality lives. AI designs the happy path. Humans design the moments that matter: when something goes wrong, when the user is waiting, when there's nothing to show yet.

### Typography as Statement
Oversized, confident headings that own the page. Tiny metadata that respects hierarchy. A serif where sans-serif was expected. The font choice alone should tell you something about the brand — if it doesn't, no decision was made.

## Photography That Feels Human

### Imperfect Moments
Candid over posed. Motion blur over tack-sharp. A subject caught mid-laugh over a composed portrait. The imperfection IS the proof of reality.

### Environmental Context
Real places with real mess. A desk with actual papers. A coffee shop with other customers. The background tells a story that AI can't fabricate — it grounds the image in a real place and time.

### Photographic Artifacts
Film grain, lens flare, slight overexposure, chromatic aberration, shallow DOF with actual bokeh — real photos have the fingerprints of real optics. These artifacts are signs of a physical camera in a physical space.

### Unflattering but True
Not every angle is the best angle. Not every light is golden hour. Real photography includes harsh shadows, awkward crops, and subjects who aren't model-beautiful. The imperfection builds trust.

## Social Content That Feels Human

### One Idea, Not Ten
Humans obsess over one thing at a time. A post about a single specific frustration, a single surprising discovery, a single moment of clarity. AI generates comprehensive overviews.

### Temporal Specificity
"Yesterday in our team sync..." "Last Tuesday when the API went down..." "I've been thinking about this since the conference in March..." Real content is anchored in time. AI content floats in an eternal present.

### Reaction to Real Events
Responding to a specific article, tweet, conversation, or experience that happened recently. Not a general take on a general topic — a specific reaction to a specific stimulus.

### Incomplete Thoughts
Not everything needs to be a polished insight. "I don't have an answer yet, but here's what I'm noticing..." is more human than a tidy 5-point framework. Humans think in public. AI only publishes conclusions.

### Your Actual Voice
Record yourself explaining the idea to a friend. Transcribe it. Edit lightly. That's your voice. It has filler words, restarts, and tangents — but it also has rhythm, emphasis, and personality that AI can't replicate.

## Video That Feels Human

### Environmental Sound
Room tone, traffic, HVAC hum, keyboard clicks, a dog barking in the background. AI video is either silent or has perfectly generated ambient sound. Real video has the chaotic audio of real spaces.

### Camera Movement
Handheld micro-movements, slight overcorrections on pan, a zoom that's slightly too fast. Tripod shots that still have micro-vibration. Gimbal footage that follows a subject with slight delay. Real cameras are operated by humans with imperfect motor control.

### Jump Cuts and Mistakes
Real video includes the part where you lose your train of thought, start over, or laugh at yourself. Leaving a small mistake in the final cut is a trust signal.

### Lighting Imperfection
One side of the face in shadow. A window blowing out behind the subject. Fluorescent flicker. Mixed color temperatures. Real lighting is what happens in real rooms, not what's optimized in a prompt.

## The Meta-Pattern

Across all media, authenticity comes from the same source: **evidence that a specific human, in a specific context, made specific choices.** AI can mimic the surface of any style. What it can't mimic is the accumulated evidence of lived experience, real constraints, and intentional decisions.

The goal isn't to be anti-AI. It's to be so specifically, intentionally, recognizably human that AI-generated alternatives feel hollow by comparison.
